02 / ASSESSMENT FRAMEWORK

Seven dimensions of scrutiny.

A common assessment lens makes the four tools easier to compare without pretending they perform the same job.

01

Purpose & capabilities

Intended use, core functions, limitations and fit for the task.

Scope
02

Reliability & performance

Accuracy, consistency, failure modes and output quality.

Quality
03

Safety & misuse

Potential harms, misuse pathways and safeguards.

Safety
04

Privacy & data handling

Data inputs, retention, processing and user controls.

Data
05

Transparency & accountability

Disclosure, traceability, provider information and responsibility.

Trust
06

Human oversight & control

Review points, permissions, reversibility and escalation.

Control
07

Organisational suitability

Use-case fit, workforce impact, governance and monitoring needs.

Adoption
03 / EVIDENCE & METHOD

Show the evidence. State the limits.

Conclusions will be traceable to their source and separated from assumptions or unknowns.

01

Provider statement

Information published by the tool provider.

02

Independent source

External research, standards or credible reporting.

03

Project observation

What was directly observed in a documented test.

04

Inference / unknown

Reasoned interpretation or information not verified.

PROJECT BOUNDARIES

Practical, not performative.

This is an educational portfolio assessment, not a legal opinion, certification, compliance audit, penetration test or security audit. Testing uses non-sensitive inputs and does not bypass safeguards or connect live systems without authorisation.

Testing methodology ↗
THE OBJECTIVE

Not “Which AI is best?”
But “When is it appropriate to use?”

The final output will be a comparative assessment, a risk register and practical adoption recommendations, grounded in documented evidence.
